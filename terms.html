The agreement for using Tapalift

Effective 23 October 2026 · replaces the version of 17 October 2026

In short. Tapalift helps people who already know each other ask for and give lifts. It isn't a taxi, a ride-hailing service or an emergency service. Circles are private, and we don't vet anyone who uses Tapalift: whoever sets up a circle chooses who's in it, and a Family circle is set up by a parent or guardian (or an adult they ask), who is responsible for it and for how location is used in it. Under 16s join other circles only with a parent's yes. Tapalift Plus is billed by the App Store and can be cancelled any time. Nothing in these terms takes away your legal rights as a consumer.

2. The service

Tapalift is an app for iPhone and Apple Watch, made by iSafeNet, for asking the people in your circles for lifts and giving them, sharing where you are with them, and planning trips together. Circles are kept on Tapalift's own service, end-to-end encrypted so that only the phones in the circle can read them, not us. How Tapalift handles information is in the Privacy Policy.

3. Lifts are between you and your people

Tapalift passes lift requests and answers between people who invite each other. It has no messages of its own: people text and call each other in their phone's own apps. It doesn't provide transport, employ or check drivers, or arrange payment. Whether to ask, offer or accept a lift, and everything about the journey (including the driver's licence, insurance, the car's roadworthiness, child car seats and the law on carrying passengers), is up to the people involved. Tapalift isn't a party to any lift or arrangement between you, and isn't a transport provider. Don't use Tapalift to offer lifts for payment that would need a licence.

Circles are private. A circle is a private group for people who already know each other: nobody can find it or join it without an invite from someone in it. Each invite is for one person: a link works once and stops after a day, and whoever made it can cancel it before it's used. The person who set up a circle can take anyone out at any time. Only invite, join and ride with people you know and trust. Tapalift doesn't check who people are, their age, or whether they're fit to drive. Vehicle details, registration plates, and driver photos are entered locally by users and sent from the driver's phone, encrypted so only the people in the lift can read them; they are not audited or verified by iSafeNet. Passengers must physically verify that the arriving vehicle and driver match the details displayed on screen before entering the car. When a child (or someone with Care & Support) gets a lift outside their family, Tapalift gives the driver a word for the pickup: if the driver doesn't know it, don't get in. Checking who you add and who you travel with is up to you and your circle.

Directions, pickup orders, times and arrival estimates are guides worked out from map data, and can be wrong. Drive safely and follow the road, the law and the signs, not the app. Don't use Tapalift while driving except in ways your local law allows.

4. Safety, and emergencies

Tapalift isn't an emergency service. Help alerts the people in your Family circle (or, if you don't have one, everyone in your circles); it doesn't contact the emergency services, and alerts depend on phones, networks and Apple's services working. If you're in danger, call 999 (or your local emergency number). Location, watched journeys and alerts help a family keep in touch; they don't replace supervising children appropriately, and can be late or missed if a phone is off, out of signal, out of battery, or has location switched off.

5. The Family circle, assisted care, and who's responsible

Who sets it up. A Family circle is set up by a parent or guardian with responsibility for the children in it, or by an adult coordinator managing care for an older or vulnerable family member (such as an individual managing injury, disability, dementia, or mental health challenges), aged 18 or over. Setting one up, you confirm that's you.

Who's in it. The person who sets it up invites whoever they choose: their children, immediate and extended family, close family friends, or dependents requiring assistance. Each person they invite can see what the circle shows them, including, where the circle's settings allow, where its children or vulnerable adults are. To maximise privacy and data minimisation, profile photography functions may be disabled within designated Family circles where real-world familiarity already exists.

Who's responsible. iSafeNet doesn't vet, check or approve anyone who uses Tapalift, or anyone invited into a circle. Tapalift provides notifications and real-time location monitoring tools as a software utility; it does not provide professional medical care, chaperoning, or emergency dispatch. The person who set up a circle, and the managers in a Family circle, are responsible for who they invite, for the circle's settings, and for how location sharing and monitoring are used in it, including making sure it's appropriate for each child or vulnerable adult, and lawful where they live. Only share someone's location with their knowledge and, where needed, their permission (or via appropriate legal authority, such as a Power of Attorney). Only add a child or vulnerable adult to a Family circle if you're their parent, guardian, legal representative, or have their agreement. Don't use Tapalift to follow anyone without their knowledge. Children and dependent adults are always shown who can see where they are.

Care & Support. For an adult in a Family circle who needs extra support (because of age, injury, disability or a condition such as dementia), a parent or another adult in the circle can turn on Care & Support, so that the carers they choose hear about that person's lifts and see where they are during them. You may only turn it on if the person has given you their explicit consent (they're then asked to agree on their own phone, and can turn it off whenever they like), or if you hold a Lasting Power of Attorney for health and welfare for them (or a welfare power of attorney in Scotland), or are their court-appointed deputy or guardian. Turning it on, you confirm which applies; iSafeNet doesn't check it, and you're responsible for it being true and for using Care & Support in the person's best interests and in the least restrictive way. The person is always told it's on and who their carers are, and you'll be asked every 6 months whether it's still needed.

6. Who can use Tapalift

Anyone with an Apple Account can use Tapalift. Tapalift asks how old you are when you start, using the age range your iPhone shares where it can (a parent sets it for a child's Apple Account), or your birthday. Under 13s use Apple Accounts managed through Apple's Family Sharing. If you're under 18, a parent or guardian must agree to these terms, and to any Tapalift Plus subscription, for you.

Under 16s use Tapalift in a Family circle set up by a parent or guardian. They can't start circles, and they join any other circle only once a parent or guardian in their Family circle says yes. Outside the family, their location is seen only by someone giving them a lift, on the way to them. At 16 and 17 they can join circles and choose to share where they are in them, and their parents are told. A parent or guardian decides when they're asked before a child's lift with someone outside the family.

9. Reporting and complaints

Reporting something. If someone in a circle, or anything you see in Tapalift, worries you or seems unlawful, use Your settings › Privacy and safety › Report a concern, or Report this photo on a lift card (it's hidden from you at once and taken off the driver's card). A report is an email from your own account to info@isafenet.app, so you see everything it sends. You can also email us directly. Circles are encrypted end to end so that we can't read them: we see only what you send us.

Blocking, and words we don't allow. You can block anyone in your circles (on their name, on a lift's … menu, or in Your settings › Privacy and safety › Blocked people): they can't ask you for lifts, you don't see their requests or photo, and they don't see where you are. A lift already on its way carries on, so nobody is left stranded. In a Family circle, children can't block, and nobody can block a child; a parent is told when a child blocks someone in another circle. Tapalift has no messages or chat, so the only words people type that others see are names, place labels, guests' names and a car's description; Tapalift checks these on your phone and won't take offensive words. Nothing you type is logged or sent to us for this.

What we do. We read every report and reply within 24 hours. We'll tell you how to leave the circle or (if it's yours) remove the person, and we can stop a phone from using Tapalift's service. Where someone may be at risk of harm, or the law requires it, we pass on what you sent us to the police or the relevant authority. If you're in danger, call 999.

Complaints. You can complain to us about anything you see in Tapalift, about how we dealt with a report, about action we took that affects you, about the age check (if you think it got your age wrong), or about how we're meeting our duties under applicable UK laws or data protection law. Email info@isafenet.app with "Complaint" in the subject; we'll reply within 14 days, saying what we've done and why. If you're unhappy with how we handle your personal information, you can also complain to the Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk).
