Privacy policy

Your circle is yours

We can't see your circles or anyone's location, and we don't collect any of your information.

Updated 18 October 2026

In short. Tapalift is made by iSafeNet (James Bulgo, United Kingdom). Everything Tapalift stores lives in a circle, on Tapalift's own service, encrypted end to end on your phone so that only the phones of the people in the circle can read it. We can't. Your photo and your car stay on your own phone. Tapalift has no messages or chat of its own: texts and calls go through your phone's own apps. The service knows a random ID for your phone (so it can wake it with an alert) and which phone wrote each encrypted record, but not who you are or what's inside (see Tapalift's service). Delete my Tapalift data takes you out of every circle and deletes it all (section 13). There's no Tapalift account, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking.

1. What Tapalift stores, and where

For a plain summary of what other people can see, and when, see Who sees what.

Each circle is kept on Tapalift's own service, encrypted end to end on each phone (with the MLS standard) with keys only the circle's phones hold. iSafeNet can't open it. A lift's exact places and live positions are encrypted again for just that lift's people, and where someone is or has been is encrypted only for the phones allowed to see them. You can be in several circles; each is separate, and only its own people see it.

  • Your name and whether you give lifts. In a Family circle, you can also have a family name (like Mum, Mam or Grandma) that's used there only.
  • Your photo and your car, if you add them, stay on your own phone, not in any circle. A photo is taken with the front camera inside Tapalift (never from your photo library), and kept only if Apple's Vision finds exactly one face in it, on your phone. When you're driving someone, your photo, car and number go, encrypted, to the people in that lift only (on Tapalift's service), for that lift, and are forgotten when it ends. Anyone you're driving can report your photo: it's hidden from them at once, taken off your card, and you're asked for a new one.
  • Your avatar, if you build one: only the choices you made (a style, hair, eyes, glasses, colours and so on), never a picture. It's kept, end-to-end encrypted, with your name in each of your circles, and each phone draws it from artwork built into Tapalift, so nothing is fetched from anywhere. Without one, people see your first letter.
  • How old you are. When you start, Tapalift asks how old you are, using the age range your iPhone shares if it can (Apple's Declared Age Range: a parent sets it on a child's account), or your birthday. It stays on your phone, and only the age band counts (under 16, 16 to 17, 18 and over).
  • Your phone number, only if you add it in You, so you can be called, FaceTimed or texted: Tapalift shows it to your family on your card, and in your other circles only to the person in a lift with you, while the lift is on. It's end-to-end encrypted with your other settings for the circle (stopping or blurring your location, Battery Saver, and who you've blocked). Calls and texts go through your iPhone's Phone, FaceTime and Messages apps, not through Tapalift.
  • Who you've blocked. When you block someone, it's saved with your own details in that circle, so every phone in the circle can keep you apart: they can't ask you for lifts and don't receive where you are. That means the circle's phones can tell who you've blocked; Tapalift doesn't tell the person, but they may notice. When a child blocks someone in another circle, their parents are told in the Family circle. Unblocking takes it away.
  • The words you type that others see (names, place labels, guests' names, a car's description) are checked on your phone for offensive words before they're used. The check happens only on your phone: nothing you type is logged or sent to us for it.
  • Reports you choose to send. Use You › Report a Concern. A report is an email from your own email account to info@isafenet.app: the reason, anything you write, and the name of the person and circle. You see all of it before you send it, and nothing is sent otherwise. We read every report and reply within 24 hours.
  • When you're free to give lifts ("free until 10pm", "not free today"), until it ends.
  • Lift requests: who asked whom, the pickup place, the time it's wanted, and what happened (on the way, picked up, cancelled), and a thank-you if the rider sends one. On Tapalift's service the circle sees only roughly where the pickup is (the area, to about a kilometre, like "Near Camden Town"); the exact place, and where you're going, go only to whoever says yes.
  • The driver's and the rider's positions once someone's on the way, so the two of them can see each other: the driver's from "I'm on my way" until the lift ends, the rider's until they're picked up (for a lift later, from half an hour before). They're encrypted for the driver's and rider's phones only (and whoever asked for the rider). This happens even if either of them has paused sharing or chosen not to show their location to the other, and both are told. The positions are then removed from the request, and each person's own setting comes back. iOS shows its location indicator the whole time.
  • A word for the pickup. When a child (or someone with Care & Support) asks for a lift outside their Family circle, their phone picks a plain word from a fixed list (like "Mango"). It's encrypted for that lift's people only, with the exact pickup, so only the driver who said yes receives it; the rider asks for it before getting in. It's forgotten at the pickup.
  • A note for the driver, if you pick one when asking ("I'm at the side gate"): chosen from a short list, never typed.
  • Widgets: the Family, Who's Home and Family Map widgets show what Tapalift already shows you: each person's line (like "At School since 08:47"), who's home, and a picture of the family map. Tapalift keeps that on your iPhone, in a space only Tapalift and its widgets can read, and draws the map picture with Apple Maps (only the area of the map is sent to Apple, not anyone's name). A child whose location needs Face ID is never in a widget, and the Lock Screen widget hides its words until your iPhone is unlocked.
  • Settings kept on your phone only: your look (Sunny or Clear), Easier to see, Shake to ask for a lift, the intro having played, and your notification choices. They aren't sent anywhere, and nobody else sees them.
  • When to leave for a lift you've promised for later: your iPhone asks Apple Maps how long the drive is from where you are to the pickup, and reminds you when to set off. It isn't shared.

Tapalift's service

Tapalift's service runs on Cloudflare, for iSafeNet. Everything about your circles reaches it already encrypted on a phone, so it can't read names, places, lifts or locations. What it does keep, so it can work:

  • A random ID for your phone (made from a key your phone creates, which never leaves it), the key's public half, whether it's an iPhone or iPad, and when it was last heard from. Nothing about whose phone it is: no name, number or Apple Account.
  • Where to wake it: its Apple push tokens (for alerts, and for Check now), so it can be told when something in a circle changes.
  • Apple's App Attest key for the phone, so the service knows requests come from the real Tapalift app on a real device. Nothing else from Apple's check is kept.
  • Which circles the phone is in, and which phones are in each circle's groups, which of them can let people in, and short-lived one-time codes for joining.
  • The encrypted records themselves, with which phone wrote each one. That's how the service makes sure only the phone that made a record (or a phone that can let people into the circle) can change or delete it, without reading it.
  • When records are sent and how big they are, and, while a request is being handled, the internet address it comes from.

A phone not heard from for 30 days is forgotten: its ID, key, push tokens and App Attest key are deleted, and its circles drop it. Encrypted history (alerts, journeys, trips, positions) is deleted 30 days after it last changed, or sooner; what makes up the circle (its name, people, places, settings, curfews, pickup lists, friend lists, Care & Support, watched journeys, planned trips, lifts, and a child's past lifts for up to a year) is kept, still encrypted, until it's deleted in the app or the circle is deleted. Delete my Tapalift data deletes it all straight away (section 13).

Your sharing diary

Your phone keeps a diary of how location sharing went (where your phone was and when, how fast, when it saved battery, and what Tapalift did), on your phone only, for you to look at. It leaves your phone only if you send it:

  • Sending your diary to support. If location sharing isn't working as it should, you can send your phone's sharing diary to iSafeNet from You › About › Support › Send my diary to support. It's sent once, when you tap Send, and never by itself. It covers the last 7 days, with the names of people and places as the diary shows them, but never where anyone else is (that's taken out on your phone). It's sealed on your phone so only iSafeNet can open it, used only to answer your question, and deleted after 30 days, or sooner if you tap Delete what I've sent or delete your Tapalift data. You get a short reference code to quote when you email us. On a child's phone, a parent says yes first.
  • Test diaries (TestFlight only). If you're testing Tapalift and turn on Test diaries (a child needs a parent's yes too), your phone sends its sharing diary to iSafeNet a few times a day, with the names of people and places as the diary shows them, but never where anyone else is. It's sealed on your phone so only Jay at iSafeNet can open it, used only to make location sharing more reliable, and deleted after 30 days. Turn it off any time in You › About › Support › Test diaries, where you can also delete what's been sent. The App Store version never sends diaries by itself.

Circles once kept in iCloud

Before 17 October 2026, some circles were kept in the iCloud account of the person who set them up. Tapalift doesn't use iCloud any more. To remove anything left there: Settings › your name › iCloud › Manage Storage (or Apps Using iCloud) › Tapalift.

2. Your location

Tapalift uses your location only:

  • when you ask for a lift: one reading for the pickup, which you can move on the map before sending;
  • while you're on your way to pick someone up, shared with them until the lift ends, and while someone's on the way to pick you up, shared with them until you're picked up;
  • on a planned trip, only if you agree when you answer: shared with your own driver from 30 minutes before pickup, and on the way home, until the trip ends;
  • when you hold for Help: your family sees exactly where you are for the next hour, even if you'd stopped sharing. The Help screen also shows your address and the emergency numbers for the country you're in, looked up with Apple Maps;
  • on Watch me home: from when you start it until you get there (2 hours at most), shared only with the people you chose to watch you (see Watch me home);
  • in a Family circle, if you're a child in it, or a parent who has chosen to share (see below);
  • on a lift outside the family, if you're a child in a Family circle or have Care & Support: from the pickup until you arrive (at most 3 hours), your own phone compares where it is with a driving route it asks Apple Maps for. The route and the comparison stay on your phone, and the driver's circle sees nothing new. If the lift goes well off the way, stops for 10 minutes, or takes much longer than expected, your phone tells your parents (or carers) in the Family circle, sends them a fresh position under your usual family sharing, and asks you "Is everything OK?". It uses only the readings your family sharing already takes.

Outside a Family circle, nobody sees where you are unless you choose. In any circle, its settings' Where you are lets you show that circle roughly (to about a kilometre) or exactly where you are, pause it, and choose who sees it, person by person; you always see who does. Your position is encrypted for only the phones allowed to see it, so nobody else receives it. Under 16s can't turn this on; at 16 and 17 their parents are told when they do. Otherwise, outside a Family circle Tapalift doesn't follow your location in the background. In You › Your location, adults can stop sharing for a while or until they turn it back on, in one circle or every circle, and choose who in the Family circle sees them, person by person. Your position, and where you've been, are then encrypted only for the phones of the people who can see you, so someone you've hidden from doesn't receive them at all. At 16 and 17 you can pause for up to 3 hours, and sharing comes back by itself. Under 16, a child's family always sees where they are.

Stop sharing everywhere. In You › Support › Worried about who knows where you are?, an adult can stop sharing their location in every circle at once. Their last position is replaced by a blank one (no place and no time), and nobody is told: others see that their location is off, but not when or why. It stays off until they turn it back on. Help still works if they press it. The page then shows who can see you now, circle by circle, with Hide for anyone, and helps you leave a circle. At 16 and 17 the same button pauses sharing for 3 hours, and their family is told, as with any pause.

Told when someone checks on you. When someone taps Check now on you, your phone shows a quiet notification ("Sam checked where you are"), at most once every 30 minutes for each person, and never for a map just refreshing. It's worked out on your phone; nothing extra is sent.

3. The Family circle

One of your circles can be a Family circle, with parents (or carers) and children (under 18, from their date of birth). In a Family circle, Tapalift also stores, end-to-end encrypted in the circle:

  • Where each child is: their latest position, how sure the phone is of it, whether they're walking, cycling or in a vehicle, their phone's battery level, whether it's saving battery, and which family place they're at. Parents can choose to share their own location too; it's optional.
  • Places the family adds (a name, a point and a size), and arrive and leave alerts for them.
  • Journeys: each journey's route (simplified), times, distance and speeds, worked out on the person's own phone, kept for 30 days, and only while they share where they are (in a rough area, only the places and times; while paused, nothing). A child's journeys (and a 16 or 17 year old's) are seen by their parents. A grown-up's journeys are private to them unless they choose otherwise: by default they're sealed for that person's own phones only, so nobody else's phone receives them. In You › Privacy and safety › Your journeys they can share them with the family, or choose people one by one, or stop keeping them at all. When someone stops sharing their journeys with a person, that person's phone is no longer sent them; a phone that already showed a journey may still have it until it's deleted after 30 days.
  • The weekly report a parent gets each Sunday is made on the parent's own phone from those trips. Nothing extra is collected or sent for it, and children can see that parents get it.
  • Walking somewhere journeys, Check now requests, pauses and rough areas (at 16 and 17, a child can pause sharing or show only a rough area for up to 3 hours; their family is told).
  • A child's date of birth, so Tapalift knows when they turn 13, 16 and 18.
  • Who's a parent. Someone counts as a parent only if a parent made them one, from a parent's phone that can let people into the family. Only a parent's phone can change someone's role or a child's birthday, and records only a parent may make (curfews, pickup lists, places, Care & Support, watched journeys, lift rules) are signed by a parent's phone and checked on every phone. What your phone says about you doesn't make you a parent.

A child's phone shares its location in the background, including when Tapalift is closed, using iOS's "Always" location permission and motion activity. To save battery it uses GPS closely only when it matters (a watched journey, walking somewhere, Help, or a few minutes after Check now), and less again under 20% battery, in Low Power Mode or with Battery Saver on. The child always sees a sign in Tapalift saying their family can see where they are, sees when someone checks on them (a quiet notification on their phone, "Sam checked where you are", at most once every 30 minutes for each person, and never for an automatic refresh), and sees who's in the circle. Before a child joins a Family circle, their phone asks "Is this your family?", and tells them not to join, and to talk to a grown-up they trust or Childline, if they're not sure.

Where someone is or has been (their position, trips, arrivals at and departures from places, watched journeys and their steps, who they're with) is encrypted only for the phones allowed to see them, so no other phone in the family receives it at all. Help and crash alerts go to everyone in the family, by design.

Check now. So that Check now answers in seconds, even when Tapalift is closed on the other phone, Tapalift's service asks Apple to wake that phone, which sends back one fresh position, encrypted for the phones allowed to see it (only if it's sharing: not while paused or showing a rough area). The service passes it on without being able to read it.

Bringing a friend

A child asking for a lift can ask to bring up to three friends. So that a child never has to type in someone else's details, a parent keeps a list of the friends each child can bring. For each friend, Tapalift stores, end-to-end encrypted in the Family circle and seen only by the family: the friend's name, their parent's name and phone number, and, if their family has already agreed to lifts, where to drop them (a place picked on the map) and until when. Nothing is sent to the friend or their family when they're added.

When a child asks to bring a friend, a parent decides first (or, from 16, a parent can let the child ask the friend's family themselves). On yes, Tapalift writes a text for the parent (or child) to send from their own phone to the friend's parent, with a link to this website (see section 7). The friend's parent answers yes or no there, and where to drop their child, and sends the answer back by text from their own phone. Only the first answer to each link counts, and only until the end of the day of the lift. A no has no reason. The lift then carries the friend's name, their parent's name, whether they're coming and where to drop them, and only when their family chose to have them dropped at home, their parent's phone number, so the driver can text them from the driver's own phone to check where. The people in that lift's circle can see it, and it goes when the lift is deleted.

On a lift in another of the child's circles (a school run circle, say), the question and each answer are kept in the Family circle, signed by the parent's phone, and only what a lift carries (above) goes onto the lift there. Nobody in that circle sees the rest of the friend list or the family's map.

Curfews and low battery. A parent can set a child's curfew (home by a time, and which family place is home); it's kept, end-to-end encrypted, in the Family circle, and each phone sets its own alert ahead, so nothing extra is sent. A child's phone tells the family when its battery falls to 20% and to 10%; at 10% it looks up the nearest public places with Apple Maps, on the phone, and includes their names, so the child and their parents know where they might charge it.

Care & Support. For an adult in the Family circle who needs extra support, a parent or another adult can turn on Care & Support, on the basis of the person's own consent (they agree on their own phone) or a power of attorney for health and welfare, or as their deputy or guardian. Tapalift then stores, end-to-end encrypted in the Family circle: the basis, who their carers are, who set it up and when, when they agreed, and when it was last reviewed. Never why they need support: Tapalift doesn't ask about health. While it's on, the person's phone tells their carers each step of their lifts (asked, a driver's yes, picked up, arrived) and shares where they are with their carers only, from asking for a lift until they arrive (at most 3 hours after pickup), even if they don't otherwise share. The person is told when it's turned on or off and always sees who their carers are.

4. Watched journeys

A parent can set up a watched journey for a child, like the school run: its stops (family places, or a spot such as a bus stop), the days, and the usual times. Tapalift stores the journey, and on each of those days the steps as they happen (left, at a stop, on the bus, arrived), worked out on the child's phone. These are end-to-end encrypted in the Family circle.

The parent's own phone sets a local reminder for the time the child should have arrived, so they're told if the child isn't there, even if the child's phone is off. That reminder stays on the parent's phone. The child sees a sign while a journey is followed.

With someone. A child, or a parent for them, can say the child is with someone else instead of on their usual way (Grandma has picked them up, or they're at a friend's for tea). Tapalift stores, end-to-end encrypted in the Family circle: the name of who they're with (as the family calls them; that person doesn't need Tapalift, and nothing is sent to them), which family place if any, when it starts, and when they should be home by, if given. While it's on, their usual watched journey isn't followed and nobody is told they're late; they're still on the family map as usual. It ends when they get home.

Bad weather. About 75 minutes before a watched journey, each family phone asks Apple Weather (WeatherKit) for the forecast at the journey's bus stop, or where it starts. Only that spot's position is sent, not anyone's name or where anyone is. If heavy rain, a storm or snow is likely while they're out, the family is told once.

Watch me home

A grown-up, or someone aged 16 or 17, can ask people to watch them get somewhere, like home. You choose who watches, by name; under-18s can be watchers only in their own Family circle. Only your phones and theirs receive anything: where you're going (its name and position), when you set off, when you're due and when they'd be told, how you're travelling, and where you are on the way. It's end-to-end encrypted for those phones only; nobody else in the circle receives it. If you're 16 or 17, your parents are told who is watching you. Each watcher's phone sets its own reminder, so they hear if you're late even if your phone is off. It ends when you get there, tap I'm home or Stop, or after 2 hours at most; then the last update holds no position, and Tapalift's service deletes it within a day.

On your iPhone only

  • Live Activities: while someone's on the way to collect you, their name, your pickup and the minutes to go show on your Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island. It's drawn by your iPhone and isn't sent anywhere.
  • Text someone for a lift and guest links write a message for you to send with your own messaging app. Tapalift doesn't send them.
  • Add to Calendar opens Apple's own New Event screen, filled in. Tapalift can't read your calendar.

5. Linked circles

A Family circle's owner can link it to another circle (for example, trusted grandparents and friends). When nobody in the family can give a lift, the rider's phone asks the linked circle too: it saves a copy of that one lift request there (the rider's name, the pickup and the time), which that circle's people can see. Nothing else moves between the circles: nobody in the linked circle sees the family's map or places.

Only the circle's owner chooses which circles are linked, whether lifts are asked there by themselves, and how long a lift waits for an answer first (5 to 30 minutes). Their phone signs those settings, so nobody else in the circle can change them. Whoever asks for a lift, a child too, sees when and where it will be asked next.

6. Planned trips

A planned trip is stored in the circle it's planned in, end-to-end encrypted, and seen by that circle's people. It holds:

  • the trip: its name, destination, times, meeting point, who's coordinating, and who's invited;
  • each answer: coming or not, the lift needed, the pickup place, whether they can drive and with how many seats, and a history of each change, who made it and when (including "answered by link");
  • the cars: who's driving whom, and ticks as people are picked up and dropped home;
  • people without Tapalift that a coordinator adds: a name, and a phone number if the coordinator gives one, so their driver can ring them.

The school run

Families who share a school run keep it in a circle of their own. Tapalift stores there, end-to-end encrypted: who's in it, the children it's for, the days and times, who drives each way each day, days with no school, swaps asked for and who took them, and, with Tapalift Plus, how many lifts each person has given this term. It never holds anyone's location. On the day, each run becomes an ordinary lift, so every lift's checks still happen on the child's phone: who's allowed to collect them, a parent's yes for a driver who needs one, and the pickup word. A child's phone recognises the child's own parents in other circles from the phones they share with the Family circle, never by name.

7. Guest links, and this website

A coordinator can send someone without Tapalift a link to answer a trip on this website, in any web browser. The trip's details travel inside the link, after the "#": the trip's name, destination and times, the guest's name, the coordinator's name, and the coordinator's phone number if they chose to add it. Browsers never send the part of a link after "#" to the website1, so this website never receives it, and nothing is stored.

The guest's answer (and, if they choose, where to collect them and a number to ring) is put into a reply link on their own phone, which they send to the coordinator with their own messaging app. If they tap "Use where I am", their browser asks permission first, and the position goes only into that reply. When the coordinator taps the reply, Tapalift adds the answer to the trip.

Bringing a friend works the same way. The link sent to a friend's parent carries, after the "#": the child's and the friend's names, the friend's parent's name, the asking parent's (or child's) name and, if they've added it to Tapalift, their phone number, the pickup place's name, the time, where the child's going if they said, and when the link stops working. The friend's parent's answer, and where to drop their child, go only into the reply they send back from their own phone. This website never receives any of it.

This website has no cookies, no analytics and no advertising, and its fonts are served from here, not a third party. It's hosted by GitHub Pages, which logs visitors' IP addresses for security purposes2, under GitHub's privacy statement.

8. Contacts

An invite on Tapalift's service is a link (or QR code) with a one-time code: it lets one phone ask to join, lasts a day, and nobody's in until someone who can let people in says yes. The service never sees names. Where Tapalift lets you pick someone from your contacts, Apple's contact picker runs outside Tapalift: Tapalift sees only the one name and number or email address you pick, and never reads your contacts.

9. Apple's services, and purchases

Tapalift uses Apple Push Notification service (to tell phones a circle changed), App Attest (to check the app is real, as above), Apple Maps (to name and find places, work out routes and driving times, and draw trips and trails along the roads: the points either side of a gap are sent to Apple's directions service, without anyone's name) Apple Weather (the forecast for a watched journey's bus stop, as above) and Siri and App Intents (if you use them). Apple handles that under its own privacy policy. If you choose Google Maps or Waze for directions, Tapalift opens that app with the stops, and their policies apply.

Tapalift Plus is bought from the App Store. Apple handles the payment; we don't receive your name, Apple Account or payment details, only Apple's anonymous sales totals.

Now and then, after a few lifts, iOS may ask whether you're enjoying Tapalift, and You › Help › Rate Tapalift opens the App Store's review page. Ratings and reviews go to Apple, under Apple's terms. If iOS gives Tapalift a crash report, it stays on your phone unless you choose to send it to us.

10. What we don't do

No Tapalift account, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking across apps or websites, and no selling or sharing of data. iSafeNet can't read anything from the app: Tapalift's service only holds encrypted records (section 1). Your information isn't used to train anything.

11. Children

Circles are invite-only: people can only join through an invite from someone in them. A Family circle is set up by a parent or guardian (or an adult they ask), who decides who is in it. Children under 13 use Apple Accounts managed through Apple's Family Sharing.

Under 16s join a circle outside their family only once a parent or guardian in their Family circle says yes (with no Family circle, they can join only a Family circle), and can't start circles. Their phone asks in the Family circle, with the circle's name and who invited them; only a parent's own phone can answer. Parents then see who's in each of their child's circles (names only) and the child's lifts there, and the child is shown that. At 16 and 17 they can join, and their parents are told.

Tapalift's Family circle is designed to follow the UK Information Commissioner's Office's Age Appropriate Design Code (the Children's Code)3, including its standards on geolocation and parental controls: a child can't be added secretly, and is always told, in words suited to their age, when they're being shared and who can see them. Anyone can leave a circle; if a child's location is switched off, or they leave, their family is told. At 18 a child becomes an ordinary member, and their date of birth is removed.

In the Family circle, children can't block anyone and nobody can block a child: a child talks to a parent instead. When a child blocks someone in another circle, their parents are told.

Children's lifts follow the same care: under 16, a parent decides before a child brings a friend; at 16 and 17 a parent can let them ask the friend's family themselves, and is still told each time. Lifts that move to a linked circle, and lifts in a child's other circles, follow the rules their parents set.

12. How long things are kept

  • Family history (alerts, trips, walking journeys, checks), watched journey steps, With someone notes, and planned trips with their answers: deleted 30 days after they happen (a planned trip, 30 days after it ends).
  • Lifts: the circle's owner chooses how long finished lifts are kept: a day, a week, a month or for ever. Tapalift's service keeps lifts until the owner's phone deletes them by that choice, or the circle is deleted.
  • Positions: only the latest one is kept for each person, replaced as it changes, and deleted after 30 days without an update.
  • Your phone's ID on Tapalift's service, with its key, push tokens and App Attest key: deleted 30 days after the phone was last heard from, or straight away with Delete my Tapalift data.
  • Blocks: until you unblock, leave the circle, or delete your Tapalift data.
  • A friend list: kept until a parent takes a friend off, or the Family circle is deleted. A child's questions about bringing friends on lifts in other circles, and their answers: deleted after 30 days, with the family history.
  • Watch me home: deleted from Tapalift's service within a day of ending.
  • Diaries you send (to support, or while testing): deleted after 30 days, or straight away if you delete what you've sent or your Tapalift data.
  • The school run: kept while the families use it; deleted with the circle, and a swap's question and answers after 30 days.
  • Widgets: what they show is replaced as it changes, and goes when you delete Tapalift.

13. Deleting your information

  • Leave a circle, or delete a circle you look after, which removes it for everyone.
  • Delete my Tapalift data, in You › Support, deletes everything in one go:
    • In each circle, your place in it and the records about you that your phones made: where you are, your settings (with your phone number and blocks), and what happened to you there (alerts, journeys and their steps, trips, who you were with, Care & Support, pickup list, curfew). A lift you were in ends on its own, and its records go with it.
    • On Tapalift's service, your phone is forgotten: it's taken out of every circle (and any it had asked to join, with its join requests and keys for joining), then its ID, key, push tokens and App Attest key are deleted.
    • On your phone, everything Tapalift kept: circles, keys (including those shared with Tapalift's widgets and extensions), files, your lift card (photo and car), the sharing diary, crash reports, settings and notifications. Tapalift then starts again, as on a new phone.
  • A circle you set up that nobody else can look after is deleted for everyone, and you're told first. If you're the only parent who can let people into a Family circle with children, you choose first: delete it, or let another parent's phone let people in. Under 16, a parent takes your phone out of the family instead.
  • Records someone else made about you (a parent's curfew or watched journey for you, say) are theirs: they stay until they're deleted (history goes after 30 days).
  • Nothing on your phone is deleted until the service has done its part, so if you're offline you can try again.
  • Deleting the app on its own stops new updates, but leaves you in your circles until your phone is forgotten after 30 days, so use Delete my Tapalift data first.

14. Your rights

Under UK data protection law you have rights over your personal information, including to see it, correct it and have it deleted. Because everything Tapalift keeps is encrypted so that only your circle's phones can read it, we can't look it up for you: you can see it, correct it and delete it in the app, as described above. If you'd like help, write to us. You can also complain to the Information Commissioner's Office.
